PVS TTS Privacy Policy

Effective date: 14 November 2026
App: PVS TTS (Android package com.pvs.tts)
Developer: PVS Khalsa

PVS TTS is an Android text-to-speech engine. It passes the text that other apps ask it to speak to the speech engines already installed on your phone. This page explains what the app does with information.

Summary

  • PVS TTS does not automatically upload data. The developer receives information only when you choose to send a support or crash-report email.
  • The app does not request the Android INTERNET permission.
  • There are no app accounts, advertising, tracking code or automatic analytics uploads. Optional reports help the developer answer questions and diagnose problems.
  • Your settings are stored locally. Android automatic backup is disabled; device-maker transfer tools may behave differently.
  • When a version installed from Google Play checks for updates, the Google Play Store app does that work (see "Update checks").

What stays on your phone

PVS TTS saves your choices on your phone: the speaking mode, the language and voice chosen for each language, the list of speech engines found on your phone, and your settings for the call, battery and time announcements. It also keeps a short note about how the last request was routed, which holds counts and times and not the spoken text. The app opts out of Android's automatic backup (allowBackup is false in its manifest), so it does not ask Android to back this data up. Removing the app deletes this data. The Android setting "Clear storage" deletes it too. This page does not cover copies a phone maker's own transfer tool may make. If the app crashes, a short crash report (app version, time and error details) is saved on your phone, shown to you on the next launch, and leaves your phone only if you choose to email it.

Text you have spoken

When another app asks PVS TTS to speak, PVS TTS checks the writing system of the text and gives each part to the engine you chose for that language. This happens on your phone. PVS TTS does not deliberately save speech text as a speech history or automatically send it to the developer. Optional support messages contain what you choose to type and send. Older raw crash logs may contain exception details, so review them carefully before sharing.

The engines you choose are separate apps made by other companies. Some of them may send text to their own servers, especially for online voices. PVS TTS does not control or see that. Read the privacy policy of each engine you use.

Permissions

The app declares exactly these permissions: POST_NOTIFICATIONS, FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE, READ_PHONE_STATE, SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM and RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED. It does not declare INTERNET, WAKE_LOCK, QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, call log, contacts, SMS, location, microphone or camera permissions.

  • Notifications: shows a notice while speech or an announcement is running. You can refuse it.
  • Phone state: used only if you turn on the incoming call announcement, to know that a call is ringing. The app says "Incoming call". It does not read the caller's number, your contacts or your call history.
  • Exact alarms: used only if you turn on the time announcer and allow it, so announcements can run at clock times.
  • Run at start-up: starts your turned-on announcements again after the phone restarts.
  • Foreground service: keeps speech and announcements running while the app is closed.

All announcements are off until you turn them on.

Other apps on your phone

To find your speech engines, PVS TTS asks Android which installed apps provide a text-to-speech service. It keeps the names and supported languages of those engines on your phone to show them in its lists. It does not read your other apps. The Settings screen has shortcuts that open Android's own battery and accessibility settings pages. The accessibility shortcut opens Android's own settings, where you can turn the optional sound feedback service on or off (see below).

Accessibility service (sound feedback)

PVS TTS includes an optional accessibility service named "PVS TTS sound feedback". Its only purpose is to play short sounds for screen reader events, such as a click, a focus change or a scroll, so a blind user can hear what the screen reader is doing. It is off by default. It works only while your spoken screen reader is running and PVS TTS is the text-to-speech engine, and you can turn it off at any time in the app's Settings or in Android's Accessibility settings.

What the service can see: only the type of each event, which way a scroll went, and the class name of the window (for example, whether it is a dialog). It sets canRetrieveWindowContent to false, so Android does not give it screen content: it cannot read text, labels or anything shown on screen.

What it does not do: it does not perform taps, gestures or any other action for you. It does not record or store anything about these events. The app has no INTERNET permission and this service does not upload accessibility events. Separately, you can choose to send a support email through your own email app as described below. The service is protected by Android's own BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE guard, which means only the Android system can connect to it. This guard is not one of the runtime permissions listed above and it does not give the app access to your data.

Update checks

A version of PVS TTS installed from Google Play asks Google Play whether a newer version is published, using Google's Play In-App Updates library. The Google Play Store app performs the check on your phone. According to Google's documentation, this processing uses device metadata, the app version, and a list of installed modules and asset packs to decide whether an update is available, and Google states that it is encrypted, not shared with third parties and deleted after a fixed retention period (source: https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-updates, last updated 2026-01-30). The developer does not receive this information. Versions not installed from Google Play do not get update prompts.

Optional support and crash-report emails

From version 26.11.54, More options > Contact us asks for your name, reply email address and message. These fields are used only when you choose this optional support feature; you can use the speech engine without providing them. Submit opens an editable draft in your phone's email app addressed to the PVS Khalsa support inbox. You review the draft and press Send yourself. Opening or closing the form does not send an email.

The draft includes your message and a technical snapshot: installed app version and build number, device manufacturer and model, Android version and API level, report generation date/time/timezone, the latest recorded diagnostic-update time when available, fixed engine-event counters, request outcomes and numeric timing information. This technical snapshot does not contain the text you ask the speech engine to speak, raw exception messages, engine or voice names, or unique hardware or installation identifiers. Information you deliberately type in your support message is included in the draft, so review it before sending.

If the app crashes, a safe crash record is saved locally. On the next app opening, a popup offers Close or Share crash report. Share crash report opens a draft to the same support inbox, with crash-captured date/time, installed version/device details, fixed error categories, and bounded app source-file and line locations when available. The current diagnostic snapshot and draft-generation time are labeled separately from the crash time. Raw exception messages, thread names and non-app stack frames are excluded from new safe crash reports. Older records may have no safe details; those details are not invented or included from an old raw crash file.

No report is sent automatically and no daily-reporting backend is used. The developer receives only what you choose to send, including the sender details supplied by your email provider. The developer uses support messages and reports to reply to you, handle feedback, investigate errors and improve the app. Your email app and providers process mail under their own terms and security practices; PVS TTS does not control delivery, encryption, or copies retained by them. Do not send passwords, authentication codes or other sensitive information.

Older app versions may offer a separate Log reports setting and share raw crash details. Review any older-version report carefully before sending it, since exception details can contain private input. Version 26.11.54 removes that setting and does not include historical raw crash files in its support drafts.

Support retention and deletion

Support emails and reports are kept only while needed to reply and handle the issue, then deleted after the reply and support work are finished. You can request earlier deletion through Contact us in the app or the developer contact details on Google Play. This is not an automatic fixed-day deletion system. Copies in your sent mail, your email provider or other recipients' mailboxes are outside the developer's control.

Children

PVS TTS does not require an account or personal information to use its speech features. Do not include sensitive personal information in an optional support message.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with a new effective date.

Contact

For privacy questions, use More options > Contact us in PVS TTS or the developer contact details on the app's Google Play listing. You can also use this route to request deletion of support information you previously sent.
